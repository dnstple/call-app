## Build notes

- Identical layout to the previous "Everyone needs a chat" ad — same headline, subheading, illustration and header pill. The only change is the price panel and the chips.
- **`£5/call` is set as one locked-up unit** — 82px figure with the `/call` tight against it — so the price and what it buys read as a single mark rather than two pieces of information.
- **No frequency anywhere**, as asked. The panel describes one call, not a pattern.
- **Chips carry the three trust points** you listed: Real people, Approved Companions, Cancel anytime. "Real people · not AI" also sits in the header pill, since that's the differentiator worth stating before anyone reads the body.
- **"45 minutes" stayed.** Without it, £5 has nothing to attach to — a fiver for an unspecified call reads cheap rather than good value. Easy to remove if you'd rather, but the offer weakens.

## Before you post

- **£5 a call is a significant price change.** Your scope has Companions setting their own rates within platform limits, and a £5 trial call as the introductory hook. A flat £5 for every 45-minute call means Companions earn roughly £6.67/hour before fees — below UK minimum wage equivalent. They're self-employed so minimum wage doesn't legally apply, but it will affect who signs up and how long they stay. Worth checking this is the intended rate and not the trial price carried across by mistake.
- **The old £5 trial no longer differentiates.** If every call is £5, there's no "try it first" hook left. You may want a different entry offer, or to drop trial language from the other creative so the pricing story stays consistent.
- **Make sure the product matches** before this points at a live page — the £5 price and the 45-minute call length need to be what people actually see when they arrive.
